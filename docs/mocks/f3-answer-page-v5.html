In adults having inguinal hernia repair, does laparoscopic repair (TEP or TAPP) cause less chronic pain than open Lichtenstein repair?

Decision support. The clinician decides.

Bottom line

Laparoscopic repair reduces chronic postoperative pain compared with Lichtenstein repair [1, 2, 3]. The absolute benefit is mainly in mild pain [4]. One long-term trial found no difference at about 8 years [7].

Moderate certainty Consistent direction across overlapping meta-analyses, but pain definitions vary and trials are open-label.

Default choice: Offer TEP or TAPP when a surgeon experienced in it is available [1, 2]. Lichtenstein remains a sound repair when laparoscopic expertise is lacking [5].

Chronic postoperative groin painModerateRCTs and meta-analyses 8 numbersLaparoscopic repair roughly halves to thirds the risk of any chronic groin pain compared with Lichtenstein.

TEP vs Lichtenstein: RR 0.38 (95% CI 0.28–0.51) across 27 RCTs with 7,658 patients [1]. Network meta-analysis: RR 0.36 for TEP and for TAPP vs Lichtenstein [2]. At 5 years: 9.4% with TEP vs 18.8% with Lichtenstein (P < 0.001) [4]. The exception is TEPLICH at about 8 years: 7.6% vs 6.7% [7].

Why this certainty: consistent direction and large pooled effects; downgraded because one long-term trial found no difference and pain definitions vary.

Moderate to severe chronic painLowRCTs 3 numbersThe absolute difference is much smaller for pain that matters: 1.9% with TEP vs 3.5% with Lichtenstein at 5 years (P = 0.092) [4].

In TEPLICH, moderate to severe pain affected 3.8% with TEP vs 5.5% with Lichtenstein [7].

Hernia recurrenceModerateRCTs and meta-analyses 6 numbersThere is no overall difference in recurrence: RR 1.03 (95% CI 0.62–1.72) [1].

The Swedish trial found 3.5% with TEP vs 1.2% with Lichtenstein; excluding one surgeon lowered the TEP rate to 2.4% [5].
