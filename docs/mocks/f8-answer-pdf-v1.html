In adults with a primary inguinal hernia, does laparoscopic repair (TEP or TAPP) reduce chronic groin pain compared with open Lichtenstein repair?

Written by PubMed Ai (gemini-2.5-flash-lite) from PubMed and Europe PMC abstracts · 20 November 2026 · pubmedai.surgeryai.io

Summary

Question. Laparoscopic (TEP or TAPP) versus Lichtenstein repair for primary inguinal hernia: chronic pain, recurrence, wound infection and cost.

Methods. PubMed and Europe PMC were searched; 8 studies were selected (3 meta-analyses, 5 randomised trials) and their abstracts read. Certainty was graded High, Moderate, Low or Very low.

Results. Chronic pain was lower with TEP (RR 0.38, 95% CI 0.28–0.51) [1] and TAPP (RR 0.36, 0.15–0.81) [2]. Recurrence did not differ (RR 1.03, 0.62–1.72) [1]. Wound infection was less frequent (RR 0.48, 0.31–0.75) [1].

Conclusion. Laparoscopic repair reduces chronic postoperative pain compared with Lichtenstein repair [1–3]; the absolute benefit is mainly in mild pain [4]. Moderate certainty

1. Introduction

Chronic postoperative inguinal pain is the commonest long-term problem after groin hernia repair and affects daily activity and work. Both the open Lichtenstein mesh repair and the laparoscopic repairs (totally extraperitoneal, TEP, and transabdominal preperitoneal, TAPP) are widely used. This summary asks whether the laparoscopic approach lowers the risk of chronic pain without a cost in recurrence.

2. Methods

PubMed and Europe PMC (free sources) were searched for randomised trials and meta-analyses comparing laparoscopic with Lichtenstein repair. Searches run: “laparoscopic versus Lichtenstein inguinal hernia chronic pain”, filtered to meta-analyses and RCTs, 2000–2026; then by repair type (TEP, TAPP) and by outcome (recurrence, cost). Of 46 records considered, 8 were selected by study type (meta-analysis, then RCT, then prospective and retrospective studies). Only abstracts were read. Each outcome’s certainty reflects the design, consistency and precision of the evidence.

3.2 Outcomes

Chronic postoperative groin pain Moderate. Laparoscopic repair roughly halves to thirds the risk of any chronic groin pain. TEP vs Lichtenstein: RR 0.38 (95% CI 0.28–0.51) across 27 RCTs with 7,658 patients [1]. At 5 years, 9.4% with TEP vs 18.8% with Lichtenstein (P < 0.001) [4]. The exception is TEPLICH at about 8 years: 7.6% vs 6.7% [7].

Moderate to severe chronic pain Low. The absolute difference is much smaller for pain that matters: 1.9% with TEP vs 3.5% with Lichtenstein at 5 years (P = 0.092) [4].

Hernia recurrence Moderate. No overall difference: RR 1.03 (95% CI 0.62–1.72) [1]. The Swedish trial found 3.5% with TEP vs 1.2% with Lichtenstein, driven by one surgeon’s results [5].

Surgical wound infection Moderate. Less frequent after laparoscopic repair: RR 0.48 (95% CI 0.31–0.75) [1].

Cost Very low. Operative costs were higher for TEP, but total costs were comparable; the abstract gives no figures [6].

4. Discussion

Conflicting evidence. TEPLICH found no difference in pain at about 8 years [7], unlike the pooled trials [1]; longer follow-up and a stricter pain definition may explain it.

Other findings. One surgeon’s results raised the TEP recurrence rate in the Swedish trial [5]. The two meta-analyses share many trials, so they are not independent evidence [1, 2].

Applicability. Case mix: mostly primary unilateral hernias in men [1]. Cost in India: not known from these abstracts; no Indian costs were reported. Training: laparoscopic repair has a learning curve, and outcomes depend on surgeon experience.

What would change this answer. A long-term trial with a standard definition of chronic pain; a trial reporting costs in Indian practice.

5. Conclusion and clinical practice

Laparoscopic repair reduces chronic postoperative pain compared with Lichtenstein repair [1–3]. The absolute benefit is mainly in mild pain [4], and one long-term trial found no difference at about 8 years [7]. Moderate certainty Consistent direction across overlapping meta-analyses, but pain definitions vary and trials are open-label.

  • Default choice: offer TEP or TAPP when a surgeon experienced in it is available [1, 2]; Lichtenstein remains a sound repair when laparoscopic expertise is lacking [5].
  • Who may benefit: patients most concerned about chronic pain [4].
  • Counselling: fewer people have lasting groin pain after keyhole repair; recurrence is similar [1].
